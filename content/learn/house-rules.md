Every mahjong family runs a fork of the rules, lovingly patched over
generations and documented nowhere. Ours is documented *somewhere*: a
one-page rule card of unimpeachable authority (laminated; survived a hot pot
incident). This chapter is that card, unpacked, plus the table law the card
is too dignified to print. If you learned elsewhere, some of these will
scandalize you. Sit with that feeling. Then comply.

## The gameplay articles

{{< houserule title="Article 1 — Largely Hong Kong rules" >}}
The base game is Hong Kong style, exactly as taught in
[Chapters 1–5]({{< relref "/learn" >}}): 13 tiles in hand, four legs and two
eyes, Seung/Pong/Gong, flowers on. Where this card speaks, the card wins.
{{< /houserule >}}

{{< houserule title="Article 2 — Fourteen dead cards" >}}
The last **14 tiles of the wall are dead** and are never drawn. Wall reaches
the dead zone with no winner? Wash ({{< zh "流局" "lau guk" >}}), no
payments, re-deal.
{{< /houserule >}}

{{< houserule title="Article 3 — The point minimum is negotiable" >}}
Chicken hands (0 points) are legal by default and pay the base stake. A
**point minimum may be applied** — but it's declared before the first deal,
like a civilized threat, not after somebody wins with garbage.
{{< /houserule >}}

{{< houserule title="Article 4 — The payment formula" >}}
**Per person: chicken × 2^(points).** Discarder pays double. Zi-Mo: everyone
pays double. Capped at **6 points — "Spicy"** ({{< zh "辣" "laat" >}}). The
[full math]({{< relref "scoring" >}}) has a worked example and the ladder.
{{< /houserule >}}

{{< houserule title="Article 5 — The 9-cards clause" >}}
When a player's exposed melds show nine tiles of one suit, the table calls
**"9-cards!"** — and any discarder who completes that full flush afterward
**pays all debts**, all three shares, alone. You were warned. Out loud. On
the record.
{{< /houserule >}}

{{< houserule title="Article 6 — Seat priority" >}}
Direct from the card, verbatim energy: **Chinese people get seat priority,
in an effort to preserve the culture.** Guests are honored, welcomed, fed —
and seated second. This is called heritage programming and it comes with
snacks.
{{< /houserule >}}

{{< houserule title="Article 7 — Couples sit across" >}}
**Couples should sit across from each other.** Adjacent seating enables
footsie-based information transfer and no one has time to relitigate the
2019 "we weren't signaling" hearings. Across the table, where the table can
see your love *and* your discards.
{{< /houserule >}}

{{< houserule title="Article 8 — All eight flowers is a win" >}}
Collect all 8 flowers and the hand ends immediately: **automatic win at 6
points (Spicy)**. It requires no skill and confers total glory. This is the
lottery clause and it's beautiful.
{{< /houserule >}}

## Table law (non-tile provisions)

The card covers the game. These cover the room. Same force of law —
arguably more.

- **Tai-Pong is a protected utterance.** Saying
  {{< zh "太碰" "tai-pong" >}} — *"I'm thinking about ponging"* — commits
  you to nothing and warns everyone of everything. It is the table's most
  honest sentence and its finest psychological weapon.
- **Phones face-down.** Checking your phone during someone's turn means
  you're buying the next round of milk tea. Doomscrolling between hands is
  permitted; we're strict, not inhuman.
- **Wash your hands after shrimp chips.** Greasy tiles are a crime against
  the ancestors and, worse, against TaiTai's set. The good set. The one that
  came over in a suitcase.
- **Say your discards out loud.** The table plays by sound. Mumbling
  "mystery tile" energy will get your discard announced *for* you, with
  commentary.
- **Trash talk: encouraged. Coaching: forbidden.** One voice per hand. Your
  cousin's boyfriend does not need three advisors; he needs to lose
  authentically, like everyone before him.
- **A false win ({{< zh "詐糊" "zaa wu" >}}) pays the table.** Declare a win
  you don't have and you pay everyone the Spicy maximum and answer to
  "Professor" for the rest of the evening. Check twice. Declare once.
- **The wall is sacred.** Touch it out of turn and the whole table will make
  the same sound at you. You'll know the sound when you hear it. You'll hear
  it once.
- **First win is on the house.** A first-timer's first-ever win is
  celebrated, photographed, and paid double by the table. Nobody's buying
  beginner tears at this establishment. This is also how we recruit.
- **Kibitzers eat quietly.** Watching is free; opinions cost. The chair
  behind TaiTai is the best seat in cinema — act accordingly.

## Frequently argued questions

**Can we play with jokers?**
No. This isn't canasta, and it isn't the league card. We're delighted your
office plays American mah-jongg; this table predates HR.

**Is the app the same game?**
The app is *training*. It taught you tiles and turn order — genuinely great.
It did not teach you to hear "Pong" from the kitchen, or what Tai-Pong does
to a room. Graduate school is in person.

**What do we play for?**
Something that stings but doesn't scar: the chicken is a dime, a chip, dish
duty, the front seat. The stake is a seasoning, not a meal. Anyone tries to
raise the stakes past fun, cite this paragraph and reshuffle.

**What if only three people show up?**
{{< zh "三缺一" "saam kyut yat" "three lacking one" >}} is a state of
emergency, and emergency powers apply: cousins may be woken, neighbors may be
recruited, and yes, the person who "just came to drop off oranges" is now a
mahjong player. The oranges were a contract.

{{< taitai >}}
People ask why we write rules down for a family game. Because rules are how
you argue *warmly*. When it's written, nobody's memory has to win — we just
point at the card, someone says "fine," and we play the next hand. That's
family, in one sentence, if you were wondering.
{{< /taitai >}}

That's the whole constitution. You know the tiles, the turns, the wins, the

— and a seat. {{< zh "開檯" "hoi toi" "open the table" >}}!
