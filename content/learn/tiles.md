A full Hong Kong set is **144 tiles**: 108 suit tiles, 28 honors, and 8
flowers. That sounds like a lot until you realize it's just a few families of
tiles, each with a personality you will come to know intimately — the way you
know which cousin will start drama at Thanksgiving.

## The three suits (108 tiles)

Each suit runs 1 through 9, and there are **four copies of every tile**. Four
copies is the number to tattoo on your brain: it's why triplets are possible,
why four-of-a-kinds are special, and why waiting on a tile that's already been
discarded three times is a rookie tragedy we've all lived through.

### Dots · 筒 tung

Circles. Coins, technically — the old-money suit. Easy to read at a glance,
beloved by beginners, no notes.

{{< hand tiles="d1 d2 d3 d4 d5 d6 d7 d8 d9" size="sm" caption="Dots, 1–9. The 1-dot is the big rosette that looks like a decorative plate from your grandma's cabinet — the one you were not allowed to touch." >}}

### Bamboo · 條 tiu

Sticks. Strings of coins, if we're being historical about it. Count the sticks,
get the number. Except —

{{< hand tiles="b1 b2 b3 b4 b5 b6 b7 b8 b9" size="sm" caption="Bamboo, 1–9. Yes, the 1-bamboo is a **bird**. No, nobody warns you. It's a rite of passage: everyone has once stared at the bird wondering what it is. It's a 1. The bird is a 1." >}}

{{< taitai >}}
The bird is a sparrow, because the game is named after sparrows — supposedly
the tiles being shuffled sound like a flock of them squabbling. Anyone who has
heard four aunties wash tiles at full volume knows the comparison flatters the
sparrows.
{{< /taitai >}}

### Characters · 萬 maan

The literacy check. Each tile is a number written in Chinese over the
character {{< zh "萬" "maan" >}}, meaning ten thousand — so the 5 of
characters is literally "50,000."

{{< hand tiles="c1 c2 c3 c4 c5 c6 c7 c8 c9" size="sm" caption="Characters, 1–9: 一 二 三 四 五 六 七 八 九. One through three are freebies. Four through nine build character. Pun intended." >}}

If you didn't go to Chinese school, or went and retained only trauma, here's
your crash course: {{< zh "一" "yat" "1" >}}, {{< zh "二" "yi" "2" >}},
{{< zh "三" "saam" "3" >}}, {{< zh "四" "sei" "4" >}},
{{< zh "五" "ng" "5" >}}, {{< zh "六" "luk" "6" >}},
{{< zh "七" "chat" "7" >}}, {{< zh "八" "baat" "8" >}},
{{< zh "九" "gau" "9" >}}. You'll have them memorized in two games, we
promise. Losing money is a phenomenal mnemonic.

## The honors (28 tiles)

Honor tiles have **no numbers and form no runs**. They only work as triplets,
kongs, or eyes.

### The four winds

{{< hand tiles="we ws ww wn" caption="East 東, South 南, West 西, North 北 — four copies each. The little letter in the corner is training wheels; use it proudly." >}}

Winds matter twice: each **seat** has a wind, and each **round** has a wind.
Chapter 3 covers both. A pong of your own seat's wind or the round's wind
scores extra. A pong of a random wind scores nothing and takes up real
estate — the decorative throw pillow of mahjong hands.

### The three dragons

{{< hand tiles="dr dg dw" caption="Red 中, green 發, and white — four copies each. The white dragon is the blank-looking one with the border. It's not a misprint. It's minimalism." >}}

- {{< zh "中" "zung" "center" >}} — the red dragon. Big red character, big
  main-character energy.
- {{< zh "發" "faat" "prosper" >}} — the green dragon, as in
  {{< zh "發財" "faat coi" "get rich" >}}. The tile equivalent of a lucky red
  envelope.
- The **white dragon** — a frame around nothing. Represents purity, or a
  blank check, or the void.

Any pong of any dragon scores a bonus. Collect all three and
you've made one of the most feared hands in the game. People will tell this
story about you.

## The flowers (8 tiles)

{{< hand tiles="f1 f2 f3 f4 p1 p2 p3 p4" size="sm" caption="Four seasons 春夏秋冬 and four plants 梅蘭菊竹, one copy each. Pretty, useless for building, worth bonus points anyway." >}}

Flowers don't go in your hand. When you draw one, you set it aside face-up,
draw a replacement tile from the back of the wall, and collect bonus points
at the end if you win: one for each flower whose number matches your seat,
one for holding **all four of a set** — all seasons, or all plants; on our
tiles you can tell the sets apart by the color of the little corner
numbers — and one for having no flowers at all.

{{< houserule >}}
We play **with** flowers, and with the lottery clause attached: collect
**all eight flowers** and the hand ends on the spot. Automatic win, maximum
points, no melds, no eyes, no notes. We did not come here to live a life
without small unearned windfalls.
{{< /houserule >}}
