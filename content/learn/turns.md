The engine of mahjong is almost insultingly simple: **draw one tile, discard
one tile.** Your hand is always 13 tiles between turns. Everything spicy in
this game happens in the space around that loop — when somebody else's trash
turns out to be your treasure.

## The basic loop

Play moves counterclockwise. On your turn:

1. **Draw** the next tile from the wall.
2. **(Optional)** declare a kong or a win if you've got one — details below.
3. **Discard** one tile, face-up in the middle, and — this is etiquette with
   the force of law — **say its name out loud.** "Three bamboo." "West wind."
   The table plays by ear as much as by eye; your popo hasn't looked at the
   discard pile since 1997 and she's still winning.

If you drew a flower: lay it out, take a replacement from the wall's tail,
carry on. Flowers never count as your draw.

## The three calls (a.k.a. legal theft)

When *someone else* discards a tile you want, you can claim it by shouting the
right word. This is the part of mahjong that makes it a contact sport.

### 上 Seung — take it for a run

If the player **directly to your left** discards a tile that completes a run
in your hand, call it, take the tile, and lay the completed run face-up in
front of you. Left neighbor only. Runs are local commerce.

{{< hand tiles="b4 b6 | b5*" caption="You hold 4 and 6 of bamboo; your left neighbor tosses the 5. **Seung.** The run goes face-up and you discard as normal." >}}

### 碰 Pong — take it for a triplet

If **anyone** at the table discards a tile and you're holding a pair of it,
call pong, take the tile, and expose the triplet. Pong beats seung: if one
player wants it for a run and another wants it for a triplet, the triplet
wins, and the run-caller gets to practice emotional regulation.

{{< hand tiles="ww ww | ww*" caption="Two west winds in hand, a third hits the table from anywhere. **Pong.** Expose it, discard, feel powerful." >}}

A related and beloved institution from the family rule card:
**Tai-Pong** ({{< zh "太碰" "tai-pong" >}}) — the formal announcement that
you are *"thinking about ponging."* It claims nothing, commits you to
nothing, and legally obligates no one. It exists purely to make three people
clutch their racks while you decide. Use it wisely, which is to say,
constantly.

### 槓 Gong — take it for a kong

Four of a kind. Three flavors, all of which end with you drawing a
**replacement tile from the tail of the wall** (a kong is four tiles standing
in a three-tile parking spot, so you get a tile back — and with it, a fresh
chance to win on the replacement):

- **Open kong:** you hold a concealed triplet, someone discards the fourth.
  Call gong, expose all four.
- **Added kong:** you already have an exposed pong and *draw* the fourth
  yourself. Tack it on.
- **Concealed kong:** all four in your own hand from your own draws. Declare
  it, lay it down face-down-ish (ends flipped, by our convention), and enjoy
  the table's suspicion. It scores better and tells everyone nothing.

### 食糊 sik wu — the trump card

If any discard completes your whole hand, you call the win. **Winning beats
everything** — pong, seung, other people's feelings, all of it. Chapter 5 is
entirely about this moment.

## Priority, in one breath

**Win > pong/gong > seung.** Ties on a win go to whoever is closest in turn
order from the discarder ({{< zh "截糊" "zit wu" "intercepting the win" >}} —
yes, someone can be *robbed* of a win by seating geometry; no, they never
fully recover).

After any claim, play continues counterclockwise **from the claimer** — which
means a pong can skip somebody's turn entirely. When it's yours that gets
skipped: straight face, long memory.

## Exposed vs. concealed

Every claim you make puts a completed set face-up in front of you. That's
real information you're handing the table — they now know three of your
fourteen tiles' worth of story, and good players read exposed melds like a
group chat with the read receipts on. A hand with zero claims
({{< zh "門前清" "mun cin cing" "clean in front of the door" >}}) told
nobody anything — and it's the backbone of the family card's six-point
"silent fortress": four concealed Pongs finished with a Zi-Mo.

The strategic tension of the entire game lives here: **claiming is fast and
loud; staying concealed is slow and scary.** You'll develop a personality
about it. Everyone does. It will be discussed.

{{< taitai >}}
New players pong everything that isn't nailed down and wonder why they're
stuck with three exposed melds, no eyes, and a hand worth nothing. A pong is
a *purchase*, not a coupon. Before you yell, ask: what does this hand want to
be when it grows up?
{{< /taitai >}}

## Tempo rules (unwritten, therefore mandatory)

- **Draw with intention, discard with rhythm.** The game has a pulse —
  *clack, name, clack, name.* Nobody expects you to speed-run your first
  night, but chronic deliberating over every discard is how you earn a
  nickname you won't like.
- **Claims must be immediate.** The window for pong closes when the next
  player's discard hits the felt. "Wait, actually—" is not a call. The tile
  is dead to you. Light incense.
- **Touch the wall only on your turn.** The wall is load-bearing,
  structurally and spiritually.
