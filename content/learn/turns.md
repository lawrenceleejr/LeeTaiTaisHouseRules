The engine of mahjong is simple: **draw one tile, discard
one tile.** Your hand is always 13 tiles between turns. Everything spicy in
this game happens in the space around that loop — when somebody else's trash
turns out to be your treasure.

## The basic loop

Play moves counterclockwise. On your turn:

1. **Draw** the next tile from the wall.
2. Optional: declare a kong or a win if you've got one — details below.
3. **Discard** one tile, face-up in the middle. That's it. Nobody needs to
   announce what it is — the table watches the pile, not your mouth.

If you drew a flower: lay it out, take a replacement from the wall's tail,
carry on. Flowers never count as your draw.

## Taking tiles from the pot

When *someone else* discards a tile you want, you can claim it. This is the
part of mahjong that makes it a contact sport. Pong and kong you call out
loud; seung you just take.

**However you claim, the meld goes down face-up.** Reveal the two or three
tiles from your own hand first, then add the discard and lay the whole set
in front of you. Never scoop the discard into your hand and sort it out in
private — nobody can check what you had, and at this table that reads badly
even when you're honest.

### 上 Seung — take it for a run

If the player **directly to your left** discards a tile that completes a run
in your hand, reveal your two tiles, add theirs, and lay the completed run
face-up in front of you. No call required — left neighbour only, so there's
never anyone to arbitrate against. Runs are local commerce.

### 碰 Pong — take it for a triplet

If **anyone** at the table discards a tile and you're holding a pair of it,
call **pong**, reveal your pair, add the discard, and lay the triplet
face-up. Say it out loud — you're claiming off the whole table, so the whole
table needs to hear it. Pong beats seung: if one player wants it for a run
and another wants it for a triplet, the triplet wins, and the run-caller gets
to practice emotional regulation.

**Play then resumes from you.** After you pong, you discard, and the turn
order picks up counterclockwise from your seat. If you claimed out of turn,
everyone between the discarder and you simply loses that turn — they're
skipped, and there is no making it up to them. When it's your turn that gets
skipped: straight face, long memory.

A related family institution: **Tai-Pong**
({{< zh "太碰" "tai-pong" >}}) — said when you need a moment to work out
whether you want that tile. It's a request for grace, not a claim: *hold on,
let me think.* Say it and the table waits a beat for you.

### 槓 Kong — take it for a four-of-a-kind

Four of a kind, and you call **kong** out loud to claim it, exactly like a
pong. Three flavours, all of which end with you drawing a **replacement tile
from the marked end of the wall.** A kong is four tiles standing in a
three-tile parking spot, so you get a tile back — and with it, a fresh chance
to win on the replacement:

- **Open kong:** you hold a concealed triplet, someone discards the fourth.
  Call kong and expose all four.
- **Added kong:** you already have an exposed pong and *draw* the fourth
  yourself. Tack it on.
- **Concealed kong:** all four came to you from your own draws. The only way
  to turn it into a kong is to **draw the fourth one yourself** — then you
  reveal all four and take your replacement tile, which is a live chance at
  {{< zh "自摸" "zi mo" "self-draw" >}}. Holding four in hand and saying
  nothing is not a kong; it's just four tiles doing the work of three.

### 食糊 sik wu — the trump card

If any discard completes your whole hand, you call the win. **Winning beats
everything** — pong, seung, other people's feelings, all of it. Chapter 5 is
entirely about this moment.

## Priority, in one breath

**Win > pong/kong > seung.** Ties on a win go to whoever is closest in turn
order from the discarder. That's {{< zh "截糊" "zit wu" "intercepting the win" >}}
— yes, someone can be *robbed* of a win by seating geometry. No, they never
fully recover.

After any claim, play continues counterclockwise **from the claimer**.

## Exposed vs. concealed

Every claim you make puts a completed set face-up in front of you. That's
real information you're handing the table — they now know three of your
fourteen tiles' worth of story, and good players read exposed melds like a
group chat with the read receipts on. A hand with zero claims
({{< zh "門前清" "mun cin cing" "clean in front of the door" >}}) told
nobody anything — and it's the backbone of the six-point "silent fortress":
four concealed pongs finished with a Zi-Mo.

The strategic tension of the entire game lives here: **claiming is fast and
loud; staying concealed is slow and scary.** You'll develop a personality
about it. Everyone does. It will be discussed.

## Tempo rules (unwritten, therefore mandatory)

- **Draw with intention, discard with rhythm.** The game has a pulse —
  *clack, clack, clack.* Nobody expects you to speed-run your first night,
  but chronic deliberating over every discard is how you earn a nickname you
  won't like.
- **Claims must be immediate.** The window for pong or kong closes when the
  next player's discard hits the felt. "Wait, actually—" is not a call. The
  tile is dead to you. Light incense.
- **Touch the wall only on your turn.**
